Related work on planetary time and communication under delay

InterPlanet and its proposed LTX meeting protocol sit alongside a body of tools and research about Mars timekeeping, delay-tolerant networking and how teams communicate when every message takes minutes to arrive. This page lists the work we know of and says what each source found.

No affiliation or endorsement. InterPlanet is an independent open-source project. It is not affiliated with, sponsored by or endorsed by NASA, the NASA Goddard Institute for Space Studies, the authors cited below, Braided Communications Ltd, the IETF or the IRTF. None of these studies evaluated InterPlanet or LTX. Summaries are based on the published abstracts; follow the links for the full papers.

Mars timekeeping

Mars24 Sunclock

NASA Goddard Institute for Space Studies (GISS). giss.nasa.gov/tools/mars24

A long-standing application that displays a Mars sunclock and computes Mars time, including Mars Coordinated Time and local solar time at any location on Mars. Its documentation describes the algorithm of Allison and McEwen (2000).

InterPlanet's Mars clock uses the same published Allison and McEwen (2000) formula, in its own implementation.

Team communication under signal delay

Voice and text communication under delay

Fischer, U., & Mosier, K. (2014). Proceedings of the Human Factors and Ergonomics Society Annual Meeting, 58(1). doi:10.1177/1541931214581025

Three-person teams (two crew members and a flight controller) solved simulated life-support problems with or without communication delay, using either voice or text. Under delay, the authors found no differences between voice and text.

Communication protocols for asynchronous conditions

Fischer, U., & Mosier, K. (2015). Proceedings of the Human Factors and Ergonomics Society Annual Meeting. doi:10.1177/1541931215591001

Follow-on work on structured communication protocols to support collaboration between distributed team members when communication is asynchronous.

Communication protocols for space operations under transmission delay

Mosier, K., & Fischer, U. (2023). Human Factors, 65(6), 1235–1250 (published online 2021). doi:10.1177/00187208211047085

Teams of three communicated by voice or text, with or without training in the protocols. The authors describe the protocols as feasible, usable and effective, and report that they were adopted for training in NASA analog simulations.

Some outcome measures are subjective, and the authors evaluated protocols they designed.

Braiding: space/ground communication under signal latency

Fischer, U., Mosier, K., et al. (2023). Braiding: A novel approach to supporting space/ground communication under signal latency. Acta Astronautica, 207, 411–424. doi:10.1016/j.actaastro.2023.03.023

A human-in-the-loop study of Braiding, text-based software for communication between crew in space and mission support on Earth under signal latency. Participants preferred Braiding over texting for the quality of time-delayed communication.

The comparison was with ordinary texting, and preference is a subjective measure. Some of the authors are associated with the vendor, Braided Communications, so this is not an independent evaluation.

Products

Space Braiding

Braided Communications Ltd. braided.space

A commercial messaging product for communication under signal delay, evaluated in the Acta Astronautica study above. InterPlanet is not connected to it.

Networking

RFC 4838: Delay-Tolerant Networking Architecture

Cerf, V., et al. (2007). IRTF Delay-Tolerant Networking Research Group, Informational RFC. rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4838

Describes an architecture for networks with long delays and intermittent connectivity, such as interplanetary links, based on store-and-forward message bundles. LTX is designed to run over delay-tolerant transports of this kind, but it is a meeting protocol, not a network protocol.

How this informs InterPlanet

The studies above suggest that under delay, how a conversation is structured matters at least as much as the medium. That is the idea behind LTX: every site follows the same agreed plan of transmit, receive and caucus segments sized to the real delay. LTX is a proposed community protocol. It has not been tested in these studies or adopted by any agency.
